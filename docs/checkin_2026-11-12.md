Facts to report at the mid-project check-in, collected as the milestones are done. The milestones from the
final abstract: "data pipeline validated against The Well's released checkpoints, budgets fixed from pilot

## 1. Data pipeline validated against The Well's released checkpoints: done

Passed 2026-11-08 (HPC3 job 59224204, one A100). We loaded The Well's released CNextU-Net and TFNO weights
into our own models and scored them with our own data loading and evaluation (one-step VRMSE, field mean,
every window of every trajectory, physical units). The criterion was "ours ≤ published" (owner, 2026-11-08).

| Model | Ours: valid / test | Published: valid (paper Table 5) / test (Table 2) |
|---|---|---|
| CNextU-Net | 0.234 / 0.224 | 0.481 / 0.670 |
| TFNO | 0.305 / 0.300 | 0.857 / 0.657 |

What else was checked (D20):
* Same data as the paper: the server's files are dated 2024-12-05 (the original release), with the official
  splits (1400 / 175 / 175 trajectories) and the same normalisation statistics (`stats.yaml` v1.0.1).
* Same code source: models, normalisation and VRMSE from `github.com/PolymathicAI/the_well`, pinned at
  `e63dec8`. Our rollouts equal upstream's exactly (max difference 0.0; VRMSE equal to NumPy within 1e-7).
* The released configs equal ours, and the weights load strictly (no missing or extra parameters).

Caveats to state:
* **The paper's TFNO never used its Fourier layers.** From 2024-06-03 until upstream commit `e430160`
  (2025-11-17) the TFNO wrapper discarded the Fourier blocks' output, so the paper's TFNO and the released
  checkpoint are a per-pixel MLP (lifting → projection). With the current code the checkpoint gives noise
  (VRMSE 10.7), so it was scored with the forward pass it was trained with. Our TFNO, with working Fourier
  layers, is a different and stronger model. Upstream issue #49 (open) acknowledges outdated paper numbers.
* **The published values could not be reproduced exactly, only beaten.** Emulating the paper's protocols
  gives, for CNextU-Net and TFNO: validation (20 shuffled batches, normalised units) 0.70 and 0.68; test with
  the v1.0.1 unnormalised-test bug 9.99 and 1.18. Neither matches the published numbers.
* Data loading had to be rewritten for speed (D23); samples are bitwise identical to upstream's.

### What the paper reports

Ohana, McCabe et al. (Polymathic AI), "The Well: a Large-Scale Collection of Diverse Physics Simulations for
Machine Learning", NeurIPS 2024 Datasets and Benchmarks track; arXiv:2412.00568v2 (21 Feb 2025). Numbers
for `rayleigh_benard` (FNO and U-net given for context; our study uses CNextU-Net and TFNO):

| `rayleigh_benard` | FNO | TFNO | U-net | CNextU-net |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One-step VRMSE, test (Table 2) | 0.8395 | **0.6566** | 1.4860 | **0.6699** |
| One-step VRMSE, validation (Table 5) | 0.6049 | **0.8568** | 0.8448 | **0.4807** |
| Best learning rate (epoch of best validation) (Table 6) | 1e-4 (32) | **1e-4 (31)** | 1e-4 (29) | **5e-4 (12)** |
| Rollout VRMSE, steps 6–12 / 13–30, test (Table 3) | >10 / >10 | **>10 / >10** | >10 / >10 | **>10 / >10** |

* VRMSE is averaged over the fields; 1 means "as good as predicting the target's spatial mean" (paper).
* One-step scores use sliding windows of the true trajectory. Rollouts always start at the beginning of the
  simulation (paper, Section 4), where the fluid is nearly still and VRMSE is ill-conditioned; every model
  scores >10 on RB. Our rollouts start later (frames 20, 80, 140; D15), so they are not comparable.
* Test results are for the run with the best validation VRMSE (paper, Section 4 and E.1).

Training protocol (Appendix E.1, E.2):
* Models scaled to about 15–20M parameters. TFNO: 16 modes, hidden 128, 4 blocks. CNextU-net: 7×7 filters,
  initial dimension 42, 2 blocks per stage, 4 up/down stages, 1 bottleneck block. Both equal our `full`
  configs.
* 4 input frames, next-frame prediction; MSE averaged over fields and space; single precision (fp32).
* AdamW; learning-rate search over {1e-4, 5e-4, 1e-3, 5e-3, 1e-2}, selected by validation VRMSE.
* Every run time-limited to 12 h on one NVIDIA H100.
* Boundary conditions handled naively: Fourier layers assume periodicity, convolutions zero-pad.

Where the paper's text and its code differ (code read on GitHub at tag v1.0.1 and at our pinned `e63dec8`;
both say the same):
* Weight decay: the text says "the PyTorch default WD of .01"; `configs/optimizer/adam.yaml` has 1e-4. We
  use the code's 1e-4.
* Batch size: the text says it was "chosen to maximize GPU memory consumption"; `configs/data/rayleigh_benard.yaml`
  has 64. We use 64.
* The text doesn't mention that the TFNO's Fourier blocks were skipped (D20).

Observations:
* For TFNO, validation (0.857) is worse than test (0.657); for FNO it's the reverse (0.605 vs 0.840). The
  paper doesn't explain it. It may come from the short, noisy validation (20 random batches, D20).
* The paper selects the learning rate on validation VRMSE, and so does our search (owner, 2026-11-08; D7).

From our own checks:
* Both repositories were created on 2025-03-28. The paper's tables already contain these numbers, so the
  training itself presumably happened in 2024.
* The TFNO checkpoint only works with the paper-era forward pass, so it comes from the paper-era code.
* Batch size: the card says it was set to fill memory, while The Well's `rayleigh_benard` data config says 64
  (which we use). The exact batch of these runs is uncertain.
* One run per model with an undocumented seed: the published numbers carry no seed-to-seed spread. Our study
  uses two seeds.

## 2. Budgets fixed from pilot timings: in progress

Fixed so far:
* Budgets 1 h and 4 h of training on one A100 (final abstract).
* Batch 64 and fp32 for every model; batch 64 fits all in A100 80 GB memory (peak CNextU-Net 58.7 GB, TFNO
  44.9, AViT 7.4) (D7, D8). TFNO cannot use bf16 (D8).
* The budgets count GPU training time only (owner, 2026-11-08; D24). Storage (`/pub`) delivers ~98 samples/s
  into one node (D23): CNextU-Net never waits, but AViT could use ~170 and would otherwise lose about half its
  steps. Waiting time is logged and reported separately, with the real (wall) hours.
* Learning rates from an equal-budget search on validation (owner, 2026-11-08; D7): each model tries 1e-4,
  5e-4, 1e-3 for 20 min of GPU time on the 10% subset, seed 0, and keeps the lowest validation VRMSE (the
  study's metric, and the paper's criterion).

### Learning-rate search: results so far (2026-11-08, HPC3 job array 59374848)

4 of 9 runs finished. Regenerate everything below with `python -m rbbench select-lr` (on HPC3, or on the Mac
after `cluster/pull.sh`). It writes `outputs/lr_search/`:
* `lr_search.md`: the full table, with VRMSE per field;
* `lr_search_val_vrmse.png`: validation VRMSE against the learning rate;
* `lr_search_training_loss.png`: training loss against GPU time, one panel per model.

Validation VRMSE (field mean) after 20 min of GPU training, 10% data, seed 0, 512 validation windows. Lower is
better; bold is the best so far.

| Model | 1e-4 | 5e-4 | 1e-3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| CNextU-Net | 0.841 | **0.469** | stopped at 16.9 min by a storage error |
| TFNO | stopped by a storage error before training | 0.358 | **0.302** |
| AViT | preempted at 13.5 min | preempted at 13.0 min | queued |

Per field, for the finished runs:

| Model, rate | buoyancy | pressure | velocity x | velocity y |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CNextU-Net 1e-4 | 0.259 | 0.151 | 1.592 | 1.362 |
| CNextU-Net 5e-4 | 0.179 | 0.082 | 0.897 | 0.718 |
| TFNO 5e-4 | 0.189 | 0.080 | 0.602 | 0.560 |
| TFNO 1e-3 | 0.177 | 0.070 | 0.501 | 0.461 |

What the learning rates change:
* **CNextU-Net:** 5e-4 has 44% lower VRMSE than 1e-4 (0.469 vs 0.841), and lower training loss all along.
  1e-3 had the lowest training loss of the three until it stopped at 17 min; its validation is pending.
* **TFNO:** 1e-3 is 16% better than 5e-4 (0.302 vs 0.358). The two training curves are close; 1e-3 is
  slightly lower throughout.
* **AViT:** no validation yet. After 13 min, the training loss at 5e-4 is lower than at 1e-4 throughout.
* So far the larger rate wins for every model, and TFNO's best is at the top of the grid. A 20 min search
  favours fast-learning rates (D7). The paper picked 5e-4 (CNextU-Net) and 1e-4 (TFNO) from a wider grid
  after 12 h of training.
* The velocity fields carry most of the error (0.46–1.59, against 0.07–0.26 for buoyancy and pressure). This
  matches D20: early frames, where the fluid is nearly still, make velocity VRMSE large.
* For orientation only: our TFNO after 20 min on 10% data (0.302 on 512 validation windows) is about level
  with the released TFNO scored by our pipeline (0.305 on every validation window). The window sets differ,
  so this is not a formal comparison.

### Pilot timings (measured in the search runs)

| batch 64, fp32, one A100 | CNextU-Net | TFNO | AViT |
|---|---|---|---|
| GPU seconds per step | 1.19 | 0.55 | 0.19 |
| GPU throughput (samples/s) | 54 | 116 | 332 |
| Steps in 1 h / 4 h | 3,030 / 12,100 | 6,540 / 26,160 | 18,650 / 74,600 |
| Epochs in 4 h: 10% / 100% data | 28 / 2.8 | 61 / 6.1 | 174 / 17 |
| Real hours for 4 h: without stalls / at the stall rate seen | ~4.0 / ~5.9 | ~4.0 / ~9.8 | ~4.0 / ~11.7 |

* Step times vary by less than 1% across runs and rates. They are GPU time only. The earlier profile (D7
  table: 1.42, 0.55, 0.38 s) timed whole steps with cached reads.
* Between stalls the data loader keeps up with every model on the 10% subset, including AViT at ~330
  samples/s. The median wait per step is about 0.
* The waiting came from `/pub` stalls of 3–21 min. They hit runs on both nodes at the same moments (TFNO runs
  at ~03:26 UTC, AViT runs at ~04:28 and ~04:47 UTC): 5–30 min per 20-min run. The 02:55 errors are the same
  storage trouble.
* Budgets are unaffected because they count GPU time (D24); only real time is. AViT's 174 epochs in 4 h on
  10% data are a point to watch for overfitting.

## 3. First 10%-data results with a revised plan: not started

To add: the six 10% runs (3 models × 2 seeds) evaluated at 1 h and 4 h (one-step and rollout VRMSE, steps to
the error threshold, kinetic-energy spectra, GPU-hours, latency, memory).

Points for the revised plan, so far:
* Replicating the paper by training our own CNextU-Net and TFNO (24 h of GPU time each, ~50 GPU-h on the free
  partition, outside the study) is postponed until after the check-in (owner, 2026-11-08).
* Budgets count GPU time; runs that wait for data take longer in real time (estimate for 4 h: CNextU-Net
  ~4 h, TFNO ~5 h, AViT ~7 h), so GPU-hours rise above the abstract's 48 h of training.
* The learning-rate search adds ~3 GPU-h (within the abstract's ~20 GPU-h for profiling and tuning).
* Storage (`/pub`) stalls and errors make real time unpredictable (2–3× the GPU time on 2026-11-08). GPU-time
  budgets keep the comparison fair, but the schedule needs slack, and the jobs need resubmitting.
* If a model's best rate is the largest one tried (1e-3), a larger rate may be better still. Whether to widen
  the grid is an owner decision.
