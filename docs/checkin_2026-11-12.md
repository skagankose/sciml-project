## 1. Data pipeline validated against The Well's released checkpoints: done

Passed 2026-11-08 (HPC3 job 59224204, one A100). We loaded The Well's released CNextU-Net and TFNO weights
into our own models and scored them with our own data loading and evaluation (one-step VRMSE, field mean,
every window of every trajectory).

**Our pipeline reproduces the paper's test numbers to within 1%.** Scored as the paper did (normalised inputs,
VRMSE in normalised units, full test set; found in the job's output on 2026-11-09):

| Model | Released checkpoint, our pipeline | Published test (Table 2) | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| CNextU-Net | 0.6688 | 0.6699 | −0.2% |
| TFNO | 0.6634 | 0.6566 | +1.0% |

The owner's criterion, "ours ≤ published" (2026-11-08), uses our own scores in physical units. It passes:

| Model | Ours (physical units): valid / test | Published: valid (paper Table 5) / test (Table 2) |
|---|---|---|
| CNextU-Net | 0.234 / 0.224 | 0.481 / 0.670 |
| TFNO | 0.305 / 0.300 | 0.857 / 0.657 |

The physical-unit scores are lower only because of the units; the predictions are the same. Over the first
~50 frames the fluid is nearly still, and VRMSE's eps (1e-7) damps the velocity scores in physical units but
not in normalised units. From frame 50 on, the two agree to four digits (D20). So this criterion compares
numbers in different units; the reproduction above is the like-for-like evidence.

What else was checked (D20):
* Same data as the paper: the server's files are dated 2024-12-05 (the original release), with the official
  splits (1400 / 175 / 175 trajectories) and the same normalisation statistics (`stats.yaml` v1.0.1).
* Same code source: models, normalisation and VRMSE from `github.com/PolymathicAI/the_well`, pinned at
  `e63dec8`. Our rollouts equal upstream's exactly (max difference 0.0; VRMSE equal to NumPy within 1e-7).
* The released configs equal ours, and the weights load strictly (no missing or extra parameters).

Caveats to state:
* **The paper's TFNO never used its Fourier layers.** From 2024-06-03 until upstream commit `e430160`
  (2025-11-17) the TFNO wrapper discarded the Fourier blocks' output, so the paper's TFNO and the released
  checkpoint are a per-pixel MLP (lifting → projection). With the current code the checkpoint gives noise
  (VRMSE 10.7), so it was scored with the forward pass it was trained with. Our TFNO, with working Fourier
  layers, is a different and stronger model. Upstream issue #49 (open) acknowledges outdated paper numbers.
* **Only the published test values are reproduced, not the validation values.** In normalised units on every
  validation window, the released checkpoints score 0.70 (CNextU-Net) and 0.68 (TFNO), against the published
  0.48 and 0.86. The paper's validation scores come from 20 random batches per epoch, with the best epoch
  picked on that draw, so they are noisy and not reproducible. The Hugging Face model cards list these
  validation values without naming the split.
* **The paper's numbers are dominated by the first frames.** On the test set, the first 20 of the 196
  windows per trajectory contribute 0.55 of CNextU-Net's 0.67 (normalised units). On developed flow (from
  frame 50) the released CNextU-Net scores 0.04 and TFNO 0.19.
* Data loading had to be rewritten for speed (D23); samples are bitwise identical to upstream's.

### What the paper reports

Ohana, McCabe et al. (Polymathic AI), "The Well: a Large-Scale Collection of Diverse Physics Simulations for
Machine Learning", NeurIPS 2024 Datasets and Benchmarks track; arXiv:2412.00568v2 (21 Feb 2025). Numbers
for `rayleigh_benard` (FNO and U-net given for context; our study uses CNextU-Net and TFNO):

| `rayleigh_benard` | FNO | TFNO | U-net | CNextU-net |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One-step VRMSE, test (Table 2) | 0.8395 | **0.6566** | 1.4860 | **0.6699** |
| One-step VRMSE, validation (Table 5) | 0.6049 | **0.8568** | 0.8448 | **0.4807** |
| Best learning rate (epoch of best validation) (Table 6) | 1e-4 (32) | **1e-4 (31)** | 1e-4 (29) | **5e-4 (12)** |
| Rollout VRMSE, steps 6–12 / 13–30, test (Table 3) | >10 / >10 | **>10 / >10** | >10 / >10 | **>10 / >10** |

* VRMSE is averaged over the fields; 1 means "as good as predicting the target's spatial mean" (paper).
* One-step scores use sliding windows of the true trajectory. Rollouts always start at the beginning of the
  simulation (paper, Section 4), where the fluid is nearly still and VRMSE is ill-conditioned; every model
  scores >10 on RB. Our rollouts start later (frames 20, 80, 140; D15), so they are not comparable.
  `eval-released` now also rolls the released checkpoints out from frame 0 as the paper did, scored in
  steps 6–12 and 13–30 (D20). On the test split, in the paper's normalised units, CNextU-Net scores 36 / 35
  and TFNO 142 / 169, so the paper's ">10" is reproduced (valid: 39 / 39 and 155 / 196). In physical units,
  test scores are 9.3 / 16.5 and 37 / 74. Velocity produces all of it; buoyancy and pressure stay below 0.5,
  so ">10" mostly reflects the nearly still start of each simulation; the models still differ (CNextU-Net
  about 4× lower than TFNO).
* Test results are for the run with the best validation VRMSE (paper, Section 4 and E.1).

Training protocol (Appendix E.1, E.2):
* Models scaled to about 15–20M parameters. TFNO: 16 modes, hidden 128, 4 blocks. CNextU-net: 7×7 filters,
  initial dimension 42, 2 blocks per stage, 4 up/down stages, 1 bottleneck block. Both equal our `full`
  configs.
* 4 input frames, next-frame prediction; MSE averaged over fields and space; single precision (fp32).
* AdamW; learning-rate search over {1e-4, 5e-4, 1e-3, 5e-3, 1e-2}, selected by validation VRMSE.
* Every run time-limited to 12 h on one NVIDIA H100.
* Boundary conditions handled naively: Fourier layers assume periodicity, convolutions zero-pad.

Where the paper's text and its code differ (code read on GitHub at tag v1.0.1 and at our pinned `e63dec8`;
both say the same):
* Weight decay: the text says "the PyTorch default WD of .01"; `configs/optimizer/adam.yaml` has 1e-4. We
  use the code's 1e-4.
* Batch size: the text says it was "chosen to maximize GPU memory consumption"; `configs/data/rayleigh_benard.yaml`
  has 64. We use 64.
* The text doesn't mention that the TFNO's Fourier blocks were skipped (D20).

Observations:
* For TFNO, validation (0.857) is worse than test (0.657); for FNO it's the reverse (0.605 vs 0.840). The
  paper doesn't explain it. It may come from the short, noisy validation (20 random batches, D20).
* The paper selects the learning rate on validation VRMSE, and so does our search (owner, 2026-11-08; D7).

What the learning rates change:
* **Every model does best at the largest rate.** From 1e-4 to 1e-3, VRMSE halves (CNextU-Net −51%, TFNO
  −50%) or better (AViT −60%). From 5e-4 to 1e-3 it falls by a further 13% (CNextU-Net), 16% (TFNO) and 23%
  (AViT).
* The training-loss curves agree: the higher the rate, the lower the loss throughout, for every model.
  None of the nine runs diverged.
* **Limitation:** 1e-3 is the edge of the grid, so a larger rate might be better still. The owner kept the
  three-rate grid (2026-11-08); this is reported, not hidden. A 20 min search also favours fast-learning
  rates (D7). The paper picked 5e-4 (CNextU-Net) and 1e-4 (TFNO) from {1e-4 … 1e-2} after 12 h of training.
* After 20 min, AViT (0.257) leads TFNO (0.302) and CNextU-Net (0.408). That is a pilot, not a study result.
* The velocity fields carry most of the error (0.39–0.78 at the chosen rates, against 0.04–0.18 for
  buoyancy and pressure). This matches D20: early frames, where the fluid is nearly still, make velocity VRMSE
  large.
* For orientation only (different window sets and training, not a comparison): the released checkpoints
  scored by our pipeline on every validation window give CNextU-Net 0.234 and TFNO 0.305 after 12 h on 100%
  data.

How the runs went:
* All nine finished. Four were interrupted and continued from their saved state: two by `/pub` errors
  (`OSError: [Errno 70] Communication error on send`, 2026-11-08 02:55 UTC; not divergence), and three AViT
  runs by preemption on `free-gpu`, one of them force-killed during a save. Resuming worked in every case
  (Gate F).

### Why 1e-3 and not the paper's learning rates

For `rayleigh_benard`, The Well's paper uses 5e-4 for CNextU-Net and 1e-4 for TFNO (Table 6), and does not
benchmark AViT. It chose each rate from {1e-4, 5e-4, 1e-3, 5e-3, 1e-2} by validation VRMSE, after training each
candidate for up to 12 h on an H100 (or 500 epochs) on the full training set. We use 1e-3 for all three
models, for four reasons.

1. **A learning rate is tuned for a training budget, and ours is much shorter than the paper's.** The paper's
   best checkpoints came after 12 (CNextU-Net) and 31 (TFNO) passes over the full training set. Our 4 h
   budget on one A100 allows 2.8 and 6.1 passes, and the 1 h checkpoint about a quarter of that. With fewer
   updates, a larger step size makes more progress, so a rate that is best after 12 h need not be best
   after 1–4 h. We therefore tuned the rate for our budget rather than borrowing one tuned for another.
2. **Equal treatment of the three models.** The study compares architectures under equal compute, and the
   abstract commits to an equal-budget learning-rate search on validation data. Taking the paper's rates
   would give CNextU-Net and TFNO rates tuned under a different protocol and leave AViT with none. Instead,
   every model went through the same procedure: rates 1e-4, 5e-4 and 1e-3, each trained for 20 min of GPU
   time (with a complete warmup and cosine decay) on the 10% subset, seed 0, scored on validation data only.
   The selection criterion is the paper's: lowest validation VRMSE.
3. **The paper's TFNO rate was tuned for a different network.** In the paper-era code, the TFNO wrapper
   discarded the output of its Fourier blocks (fixed upstream on 2025-11-17, commit `e430160`; D20). The
   paper's TFNO, and its rate of 1e-4, therefore belong to what is effectively a per-pixel MLP. Our TFNO uses
   its Fourier layers, so its rate needed tuning anyway.
4. **The data favour 1e-3 clearly and consistently.** For every model, 1e-3 gave the lowest validation VRMSE
   and the lowest training loss for almost all of the 20 min, and no run diverged:

   | Validation VRMSE | 1e-4 | 5e-4 (paper's CNextU-Net rate) | 1e-3 | 1e-3 vs 5e-4 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | CNextU-Net | 0.841 | 0.469 | **0.408** | −13% |
   | TFNO (paper's rate: 1e-4) | 0.603 | 0.358 | **0.302** | −16% |
   | AViT (not in the paper) | 0.646 | 0.332 | **0.257** | −23% |

   At 1e-4, the paper's TFNO rate, our TFNO is twice as far off (0.603 vs 0.302).

Limitations:
* **1e-3 is the largest rate we tried**, so a larger one might be better still for our budgets. The paper
  found 5e-3 and 1e-2 worse over 12 h, but we did not test them. The owner decided to keep the three-rate
  grid (2026-11-08).
* **A 20 min search favours rates that learn fast early.** Our runs last 3–12× longer than the search, and a
  smaller rate could catch up late in a 4 h run. Two things limit this. The schedule decays the rate to zero
  by the end of the 4 h budget, so late training runs at small rates anyway. And the same bias applies
  equally to all three models.
* **Comparison with the paper's own settings:** the replication planned after the check-in trains CNextU-Net
  and TFNO with the paper's rates (5e-4, 1e-4) and budget (24 h), separately from this study.
